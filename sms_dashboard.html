01

Vendor performance — Sinch vs. Syniverse

The routing question: should more traffic go to Sinch or Syniverse? The honest answer requires separating vendor effect from traffic mix — the two vendors carry different programs, message types, and eras.

Headline finding — we can't actually measure Syniverse deliverability. Delivery rate is populated for 100% of Sinch, Telnyx and even test rows — but 0 of 3,703 Syniverse parts. The single most important vendor metric is missing for one of the two vendors we're being asked to compare. Fixing this instrumentation gap should precede any routing decision.

Delivery data coverage by vendor

Share of a vendor's sends that have any delivery figure recorded. This is a data-completeness flag — not how well the vendor delivers.
Syniverse's "0%" = zero rows populated, not zero delivery. All 3,703 Syniverse sends have a blank delivery field, so its actual deliverability is unmeasured here. Sinch/Telnyx at "100%" simply means the field is always filled. Syniverse likely isn't returning delivery receipts (DLRs) into the pipeline, or they aren't being captured — until fixed, its deliverability can only be inferred indirectly (via reply behavior).

Volume by vendor

Total contacts messaged. Sinch carries ~2× Syniverse; the comparison is not balanced.
Sinch 1.07B · Syniverse 500M · Telnyx 85M contacts. Sinch also skews toward reply-heavy Experiments and Auto-Responder traffic, which inflates its raw averages.

Reply rate: raw average vs. like-for-like

Raw looks like a 2× Sinch win. Matched on the same project + month + message type, the gap nearly vanishes.
The raw gap is mostly mix, not vendor. Across the 101 tightly-matched cells (same project, month & SMS/MMS), Sinch wins reply in only 48% of them; the volume-weighted edge is ~+0.9pp. At the brand level Sinch leads in 47 of 54 head-to-head brands (+1.8pp weighted). Real but modest — nothing like 2×.

10DLC brands carry reputation and use consistent send-from numbers, so a bad brand can quietly drag delivery. Two things stand out: reply rate is a department-level story, and a handful of high-volume brands have alarmingly low delivery.

Other signals worth a decision

Two structural things jump out beyond the vendor head-to-head: an aggregator transition already underway, and how much texter model shapes outcomes.

Aggregator mix over time

Monthly contacts by aggregator. A live migration story: Telnyx wound down, Syniverse ramped, Sinch stayed anchor.
SinchSyniverseTelnyx
Routing already shifted once. Telnyx ran ~1M+/month through Jan 2025, then collapsed to negligible tails and effectively stopped; Syniverse spun up from Aug 2024 and now runs neck-and-neck with Sinch in recent months. Whatever drove the Telnyx exit (cost? deliverability? contract?) is exactly the kind of decision this analysis should inform going forward.

Volume-weighted. Auto Responder is nearly half of all volume and behaves differently.

Auto Responder (455M contacts) replies highest (5.9%) but delivers lowest (77%) — a very different profile from Volunteer sends. Vendor comparisons must hold texter type constant or Auto-Responder-heavy Sinch looks artificially strong.

Data quality notes & why we weight

Noise the "parts" table warned us about — and how the metrics here handle it.

• "100%" and "0%" single-send delivery are mostly a small-send artifact. 34% of sends with delivery data sit at exactly 1.0 and 4% at exactly 0.0 — but those have a median of 4 contacts. On a 4-message send, delivery can only be 4/4 or 0/4; there's no room for a realistic 87%. Require ≥1,000 contacts and the pile-up vanishes: delivery becomes a smooth ~85–95% curve.

• This is exactly why every rate here is volume-weighted. Averaging raw per-send rates would let thousands of 4-message test blips vote equally with an 8,000-contact real send — inflating Sinch's median to 98.6% vs. its honest message-weighted 79.5%. Weighting shrinks the tiny sends to near-zero influence.

• ~5,600 parts have <10 contacts — tests and micro-sends, the source of the boundary pile-up above.

• A "fakeservice" aggregator (32 parts, 773 contacts) is clearly test/canceled traffic — excluded from vendor rates here.

• Some brands show delivery null even on Sinch (e.g. BJAXM7M) — the DLR pipeline has gaps beyond just Syniverse.

04

What I'd do next

Where the juice is, in priority order — and the data or instrumentation I'd ask for first.

01 · Fix first

Get Syniverse delivery receipts flowing

No routing call should be made while one vendor's deliverability is unmeasured. Confirm whether it's a Syniverse DLR gap or an ingestion gap on our side, then backfill.

02 · Test

Run a real randomized split

Same campaign, same list, randomize routing at the contact level across Sinch/Syniverse. That's the only way to turn "near-parity, slight Sinch edge" into a defensible number. Observational data can't.

03 · Investigate

Escalate the sub-50% delivery brands

B8OZ4TG, BBDXYWT, BY8DGCG and peers. Pull their send-from numbers and 10DLC registration status — likely carrier filtering on brand reputation, recoverable with number/brand hygiene.

04 · Instrument

Add cost + carrier + error codes

Routing is a cost×deliverability decision and we only see one side. I'd want per-message cost by vendor, destination carrier, and carrier error/violation codes to separate "not delivered" from "filtered."

05 · Dig

Explain the 2025Q3 Sinch dip & the Telnyx exit

Both are decisions or events hiding in aggregate lines. Understanding why Telnyx was dropped tells us what "good" looked like when we last switched.

06 · Coordinate

Standardize the metric definitions

Reply rate means different things per program. I'd align with program owners on which metric is the goal per project so cross-program dashboards stop comparing apples to oranges.
